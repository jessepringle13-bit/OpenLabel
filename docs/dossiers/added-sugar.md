## 0. Claims (split)
- A. Eating or drinking added sugars above about 10 percent of daily energy raises the risk of tooth decay. Scope: adults and children, total intake, dental caries.
- B. Drinks sweetened with added sugar raise the risk of weight gain and type 2 diabetes. Scope: sweetened drinks, adults and children, about one can a day.
Not claimed: that all foods with added sugar carry the same risk; 100 percent juice; sugar substitutes (see non-sugar-sweeteners dossier).
Reviewer: draft by OpenLabel research; owner review pending.

## 5. Outcome
- A (dental caries): status converging, orange with amount (under 10 percent of energy; about 50 g at 2,000 calories, our arithmetic). Shows: positive associations; moderate-quality evidence; high certainty for sugary drinks. Does not show: per-serving thresholds; benefit under 5 percent; substitutes. Would change: trials showing no difference in decay across intake levels.
- B (sugary drinks): status converging, orange with amount (about one 12-ounce can a day). Shows: weight gain in trials; diabetes association at moderate certainty. Does not show: effects of added sugar in other foods; cause for diabetes. Would change: large trials with hard outcomes, or a re-weighing after adjusting for industry funding.
Owner decision needed: whether orange is the right color for both rows, and whether the tooth decay row should apply to all products with added sugar or only drinks.

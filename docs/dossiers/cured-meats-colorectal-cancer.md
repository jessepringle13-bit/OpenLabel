## 3. Argument ledger
- A1 (supports): IARC found sufficient evidence that processed meat causes colorectal cancer; about 18% higher risk per 50 g a day (S1). 
- A2 (supports): WCRF/AICR rated it convincing; RR 1.16 (1.08 to 1.26) per 50 g a day, 10 studies, 10,738 cases (S2).
- A3 (supports): Chan 2011 RR 1.18 (1.10 to 1.28) per 50 g a day; roughly linear up to about 140 g a day of red and processed meat (S3).
- A4 (qualifies): IARC says Group 1 reflects strength of evidence, not size of risk; no safe intake level established; evidence on cooking method insufficient (S1).
- A5 (qualifies): observational data; residual confounding cannot be ruled out; between-study differences moderate (I-squared 56% for total meat) (S3).
- A6 (qualifies): EFSA kept safe levels for added nitrite (ADI 0.07 mg/kg body weight a day) and nitrate (3.7); nitrosamines from nitrite added at approved levels are of low concern; nitrite unintentionally present in meat products might give rise to concern; highly exposed children might slightly exceed the nitrite limit (S4).
- A7 (opposes / disputes): industry calls the classification an alarmist overreach and the hazard theoretical (S6). This is a position; it cites no study in the text retrieved.
- A8 (qualifies, labeling): USDA said in 2020 it intended to rule on uncured and no-nitrate-added claims; CSPI and Consumer Reports report similar nitrite levels in uncured products (S5). Their tests were not examined.

## 4. Challenge
- Directness: A1 to A3 measure intake of the food category and colorectal cancer in people, which is what the claim states. They do not isolate curing agents. Result: holds for the category claim; does not support an ingredient-level claim.
- Quality: prospective cohort pooling, no randomized trials (considered not feasible by S3). Result: holds with confounding caveat.
- Conflicts: S3 funded by a cancer-research charity, funder had no role. IARC and WCRF funding not stated on pages read: not scored as none.
- Standing: retraction and correction checks not run.
- Relevance log: S7 mechanism review not used for any color because it is background. Production-process dose calculations in S7 are approximate by its own statement.

## 5. Outcome (provisional)
- Status: converging for the category claim.
- Shows: processed meat intake is associated with higher colorectal cancer risk across several independent expert reviews.
- Does not show: which component is responsible; size of risk for an individual; a safe amount; anything about uncured products specifically.
- Color: orange for people who watch this topic, stated amount in relative terms; otherwise grey.
- Would change this: independent evidence on what drives the association; completed funding and standing checks; reading the primary IARC and EFSA opinions.

## 7. Review round 1 (2026-10-03)
Added: EFSA 2017 scientific opinion abstract [V] (S8) and the Bouvard 2015 Lancet Oncology record (S9) [V: PubMed lists WHO and US National Cancer Institute grants; abstract not on the page].
- Standing (C1): Crossref lists no retraction, correction or expression of concern for Chan 2011 or Bouvard 2015. Crossref correction coverage is incomplete, so this is "none recorded", not "none exist".
- New finding from S8: EFSA's full opinion is less reassuring than its 2017 press summary on one point. Nitrosamines formed in the body from nitrite at the safe limit had a margin of exposure above 10,000, but nitrosamines already present in meat products had a margin below 10,000 at high exposure in all age groups, and EFSA could not separate nitrosamines from added nitrite from those in the food matrix. It also found some evidence linking nitrite plus nitrate from processed meat, and evidence linking preformed NDMA, with colorectal cancer. The record text was updated for this.
- USDA uncured rule: planned in 2020; a September 2026 opinion piece by a US House member says the rulemaking was never finished. No official 2026 status was confirmed.

## 8. Update search, post-2015 studies (2026-10-03)
Read as abstracts or extracts, not full text. Direction of evidence did not reverse.
- Agree: 2025 meta-analysis, 60 prospective studies, processed meat and colorectal cancer HR 1.21 (1.14-1.28), NIH-funded [V]; UK Biobank 2020, 474,996 adults, HR 1.18 per 20 g/day (1.03-1.31), UK MRC, CRUK, Wellcome funded [V]; WCRF 2025 dietary patterns report recommends avoiding processed meat [V].
- Dispute: NutriRECS 2019 (Annals, no external funding stated) rated evidence low certainty and weakly suggested continuing current intake [V]; Harvard critique says it contradicted its own reviews [V]. Lescinsky 2022 (Nature Medicine) covers unprocessed red meat only, not processed meat, so not used; a 2023 Nature Medicine critique of that method exists. A 2026 umbrella review rated 72% of processed-meat meta-analyses very low and 28% low certainty by GRADE [V].
- Mechanism cohorts: EPIC 2025 (367,463) nitrosyl-heme and colorectal cancer HR 1.01, no association [V]; NutriNet-Santé 2022 (101,056) additive nitrite HR 1.22 (0.85-1.75) and nitrate HR 1.26 (0.90-1.76) for colorectal, not significant, authors cite limited power; additive nitrates linked to breast and nitrites to prostate cancer [V].

## 9. IARC Monograph 114 read (2026-10-03)
Evaluation (Section 6): limited evidence in humans for red meat (Group 2A); sufficient evidence in humans for processed meat, which causes colorectal cancer (Group 1); inadequate animal evidence for both. Section 5: 18 cohorts on processed meat and colorectal cancer, 12 positive; about 150 case-control studies reviewed, about 10% informative; 10-cohort meta-analysis RR 1.18 (1.10-1.28) per 50 g/day. Mechanistic evidence moderate for processed meat; mechanisms cannot be attributed to one component. Participants: 22 Working Group scientists, no significant interests footnoted; industry observers (North American Meat Institute, National Pork Producers Council, Kraft Heinz, National Cattlemen's Beef Association, EpidStat funded by Beef Checkoff) attended without taking part in evaluations. Not read line by line: Section 2.2 tables, Section 4.

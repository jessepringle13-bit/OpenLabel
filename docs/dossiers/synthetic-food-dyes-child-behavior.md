## 0. Claim
Certain synthetic food dyes can increase hyperactivity or attention problems in some children. Scope: children; short-term behavior ratings; dye mixtures. Not claimed: that dyes cause ADHD, or any effect in adults.

## 3. Argument ledger
- A1 (supports): S1 found increased hyperactivity after dye and sodium benzoate mixtures. 3-year-olds, mix A: effect 0.20 (95% CI 0.01 to 0.39, p=0.044); 8 to 9-year-olds, complete cases: mix A 0.12, mix B 0.17. Mix B not significant for 3-year-olds.
- A2 (supports): S3 parent-rated behavior g=0.18 (0.08 to 0.29), 20 studies, 794 participants; 0.12 (0.01 to 0.23) after publication-bias adjustment.
- A3 (qualifies): S3 teacher or observer ratings g=0.07 (-0.03 to 0.18), not significant, 10 studies.
- A4 (supports): S5 concluded human studies associate synthetic dyes with adverse neurobehavioral outcomes, with variation in sensitivity; said current acceptable daily intakes rest on 35 to 70-year-old studies not designed to detect these effects.
- A5 (opposes / qualifies): S2 judged the evidence limited, effect small, inconsistent across ages and mixtures, clinical significance unknown, and did not change the acceptable daily intakes. The trial tested mixtures, not single dyes.
- A6 (qualifies): S4 concluded dyes are not a main cause of ADHD but may affect some children; FDA committee vote 8 to 6 against a ban or warning label in 2011.
- A7 (opposes): S6 says there is insufficient evidence for a causal link and cites FDA, JECFA and EFSA. Industry position; not checked against those bodies.
- A8 (regulatory context, not evidence for A1 to A7): FDA revoked Red No. 3 for food on 2025-01-15 under the Delaney Clause based on male rat cancer at high levels (S7); FDA is working with industry toward eliminating six other dyes by end of 2027, voluntary (S8). FDA statements cite concern about children but give no study results (S8).

## 4. Challenge
- Directness: S1 measures hyperactivity ratings in children given mixtures that include sodium benzoate; it cannot attribute to a dye. Result: holds for the mixture claim only.
- Quality: S1 is large and randomized; effects are small; observer-dependent. S3 results depend on who rated and show publication-bias risk. Result: holds as small, inconsistent effects.
- Conflicts: S3 is industry-partly funded and still found an effect; S1 is government-funded. Both stated. S6 is an industry position.
- Standing: retraction and correction checks not run. A Lancet correction notice exists for the S1 era ("Department of Error" surfaced in search) but was not read [?].
- Relevance log: regulatory actions (A8) are not used to set the color because they are policy choices, not outcome evidence, and the FDA pages give no study results.

## 5. Outcome (provisional)
- Status: contested.
- Shows: small effects on parent-rated behavior in some children from dye mixtures; regulators disagree on whether it is established.
- Does not show: which dye; dose; adults; ADHD causation.
- Color: yellow for people who watch this topic; otherwise grey.
- Would change this: single-dye trials, independent replication with teacher or objective measures, regulator reassessment, completed standing checks.

## 0. Claims (split)
- Claim A: higher dietary linoleic acid / seed oil intake raises inflammation in people.
- Claim B: replacing saturated fat with linoleic-acid-rich oils harms, or fails to improve, heart outcomes.
Date: 2026-09-30.

## 3. Argument ledger
- A1 (supports A): linoleic acid is converted to arachidonic acid, which makes pro-inflammatory signalling molecules, so high intake pushes toward inflammation. Source S7.
- A2 (opposes A): little linoleic acid is converted to arachidonic acid; higher intake does not raise tissue arachidonic acid and did not raise inflammation markers. Sources S4, S6.
- A3 (opposes A): randomized trials in healthy adults found no effect on inflammation markers. Source S1.
- A4 (opposes A/B): observational studies link higher linoleic acid intake to lower cardiovascular mortality. Source S5 (secondary).
- B1 (supports B): recovered Sydney trial data showed higher death rates with a linoleic-acid-rich diet: all-cause 17.6% vs 11.8% (hazard ratio 1.62, P=0.05) and cardiovascular 17.2% vs 11.0%; updated meta-analysis showed non-significant trends toward more coronary death (hazard ratio 1.33, interval 0.99 to 1.79). Source S2 [V].
- B2 (supports B): recovered Minnesota trial data: cholesterol fell but heart disease and overall deaths did not. Source S3 [?, summary only].
- B3 (qualifies B): critiques of the Minnesota reanalysis exist. Source S9 [?, unread].

## 4. Challenge
- A1: directness weak (mechanism, not human outcome); A2 and A3 test it directly and did not support it. Result: weakened.
- A3: human randomized trials but small (largest 60 completers), short (2 weeks to 40 days), healthy adults, biomarker outcomes; author has an industry-linked advisory role. Result: holds for short-term markers in healthy adults; cannot rule out longer-term effects.
- A2: sources are secondary or an advisory statement; primary not fully read. Result: holds provisionally [?].
- A4: observational, confounding likely; read only secondarily. Result: holds provisionally [?].
- B1: measures death, not inflammation; borderline significance; secondary-prevention trial; recovered old data. Result: holds for that trial and population; does not support Claim A.
- B2: not fully read; measures heart outcomes. Result: unverified [?].
- B3: unread. Result: unverified [?].
Relevance log: B1-B3 are set aside for Claim A because they measure death and heart disease, not inflammation. They remain in scope for Claim B.

## 5. Outcome (draft)
- Claim A: unsupported by current human trial evidence; not disproven. Trials are small, short and biomarker-based; more independent long-term trials are needed.
- Claim B: contested. Two recovered-data trials raise questions about heart benefit or harm; observational data and advisory bodies point the other way; critiques of the reanalyses exist and are unread.
- What would change this: large long-term trials with inflammation and heart outcomes; funding disclosures showing ties that alter how much to trust any source; reading the primary papers marked [?].
- Concern by context: no evidence found of inflammation from normal intake in healthy adults; heart-outcome question open for people replacing saturated fat with omega-6-heavy oils.

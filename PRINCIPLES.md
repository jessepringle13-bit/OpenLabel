# OpenLabel Principles

## Purpose
OpenLabel shows people what is in their food and what is actually known about it. We hold an openly stated point of view: whole, minimally processed food, organic where practical, is the ideal. That is our stance, and we label it as a stance, separate from the evidence. Everything else, including additives, processed ingredients and why manufacturers use them, is shown in full so people can decide for themselves.

## The problem we solve
People hear a claim, look it up once, settle on one side, and dismiss the other as not relevant. AI tools make this worse when they give a one-line verdict based on a few reviews. OpenLabel does the opposite: it shows the work and leaves the conclusion to the reader.

## Principles
1. Never give a single-source verdict. Every claim shows the strongest case on each side. No answer rests on one reviewer, one review or one summary.
2. Show the basis, not just the conclusion: the studies, who funded them, the design, and how strong the evidence is.
3. Say what is uncertain and what is contested. "Unproven" and "disproven" are different statements.
4. Make disagreement visible. When credible reviewers disagree, show that instead of averaging it away.
5. Relevance is not a convenient excuse. Dismissing a study as not relevant needs a stated reason (for example animal-only, wrong dose, biomarker-only), and that reason is visible and open to challenge.
6. Hold our own leanings to the same test. Our stance toward whole foods is labeled as a stance. Evidence that supports us gets the same scrutiny as evidence that does not.
7. Keep reasoning inspectable. Methods, sources and corrections are public, and any claim can be traced back to its evidence.
